1 · Anchor

video for a broad audience

Humanoid robots learn from teleoperation data. Retargeting maps every operator onto one robot body, so the data is treated as anonymous. It isn't. From the robot's joint motion alone, we recover the operator's age, gender, height, weight and identity. https://unveil-humanoid-operators.github.io 🧵

0–6 s: a person dances (motion capture). 6–12 s: the same dance retargeted onto a Unitree G1. 13–20 s: only the robot is left, and the predicted attributes appear next to the true ones. 20–26 s: a body drawn from the predicted attributes dances beside the robot. 26–30 s: closing title.

Check before posting: the actual operator in this clip is male, 22 yr, 175 cm, 76 kg. That matches BONES-SEED operator A308, who is in the training split. If the prediction (male, 25 yr, 172 cm, 72 kg) came from the seen-operator protocol, the video should not suggest that UNVEIL had never seen this person. The alternative is to switch to a clip from an unseen operator.

2 · Why it happens

one insight

Why? Retargeting matches the robot's pose to the human's, frame by frame. Nothing constrains how fast joints move between frames. So each operator's speed, range and rhythm pass into the robot data. Same routine, two operators: the 60-year-old's robot twists its waist 3× slower.

0–13.5 s: two G1 robots perform the same routine, dancing_routine_V001_003, at real speed. Under each robot, a live trace shows its waist speed over the last 4 s, with the running average as a dashed line. 14–21 s: the averages over the whole routine, 45.3 vs 14.0 °/s. This is the paper's Fig. 4 pair: A308 (22 yr) and A276 (60 yr). The speeds are recomputed with the paper's feature code (compute_multi_axis_features, the waist's 3 axes, 120 fps) and match Fig. 4 to 0.01 °/s.

Timing: the older operator took 20.4 s for the routine and the younger one 12.9 s. The video plays the first 12.5 s of each take, side by side. So the robots are not at the same point in the choreography at the same moment. The running averages at 12.5 s are 46.4 and 16.7 °/s. The closing card shows the whole-routine averages.
Confound: the two operators in this pair also differ in weight: 76 kg vs 120 kg. A single pair cannot separate age from weight. Tweet 3 shows the population result. The paper's partial correlations control for the other attributes (waist range of motion vs age, rpartial = −0.25).

3 · It holds across the population

one insight

This is not one hand-picked pair. Across BONES-SEED's dancing clips, robots driven by operators aged 55+ twist their waist more slowly than robots driven by operators aged 22 or younger. Median 19.7 vs 25.1 °/s, 29 vs 19 operators, p = 0.008.

Manim. Each dot is one operator: the median waist speed over all of that operator's dancing clips. The test is a two-sided Mann-Whitney U test on the per-operator medians.

Differs from the paper's Fig. 4: the paper plots one dot per clip. With duplicates removed, that is 832 young clips and 154 old clips. But 756 of the 832 young clips (91%) come from only 4 operators: A313, A311, A308 and A306. Counted per clip, the young median is 56.8 °/s. Counted per operator, it is 25.1 °/s. The per-clip p-value (about 10−63) treats clips from the same person as independent. That is why this cut uses one dot per operator. The effect is smaller this way but still significant.

4 · How strong the leakage is

one insight

How much leaks? 522 operators, 142K robot trajectories. Known operators, new clips: 97.2% top-1 re-ID (chance 0.2%). Never-seen operators: 83.4% gender accuracy; median error 2.1 yr age, 3.9 cm height, 5.3 kg weight. Holds across 3 retargeting pipelines.

Manim, 15 s. Part 1: re-ID top-1, 97.2% against a 0.2% random guess. Part 2: gender accuracy on never-seen operators under three retargeting pipelines (83.4 / 80.9 / 76.1%) against the 50% chance line. Part 3: median errors for age, height and weight. All numbers are from Tab. 1.

Sample size not in the video: split_summary.json lists 99 never-seen test operators and 15,002 test clips. I am inferring that Tab. 1 used this split. Please confirm before we quote "99 operators" anywhere.
Option: add a bar for the 2-layer MLP baseline, which reaches 85.3% re-ID and 79.4% unseen-operator gender. It shows the leakage does not depend on UNVEIL's architecture. It is left out to keep the video short.

5 · Why it matters

one insight

Does this matter to operators? In a 100-person survey, 73% believed retargeting hides who they are. After learning it doesn't, 63% of those willing to contribute withdrew. A first anonymizer cuts re-ID from 97.2% to 16.8%, but tracking success falls from 91.8% to 72.3%.

Manim, 15 s. Part 1: survey bars, 73% believed retargeting hides the operator and 63% withdrew. Part 2: anonymizer trade-off, with re-ID 97.2 → 16.8% next to tracking success 91.8 → 72.3% (Tab. 4).

Denominator: the 63% is a share of the participants who were initially willing, not of all 100. The paper does not say how many that was. The video's footnote states the denominator in words. If you have the count, we can add it.
