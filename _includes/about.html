Hi! I am a PhD student in Computer Science at the University of California, Irvine, advised by Dr. Habiba Farrukh in the SPECT Lab. Before UCI, I received my B.Sc. in Computer Science and Engineering from Bangladesh University of Engineering and Technology (BUET) and taught at BRAC University as a Lecturer.

My research focuses on the security and privacy of embodied AI and other emerging systems, spanning robot learning and teleoperation, AR/VR, and autonomous driving, with an adversarial machine learning lens. I study how the sensors, motion data, and learned perception pipelines these systems depend on can be manipulated by adversaries or leak sensitive information about the people who operate and use them, and I build simulation and measurement frameworks that expose these risks before deployment.

Questions I am working on
  • Embodied AI privacy: what robot learning and teleoperation data reveals about the humans who recorded it
  • AR/VR security: how a headset's own hardware can be turned against its spatial understanding
  • Autonomous driving: how physical adversarial attacks play out when a human driver is back in the loop
  • World model security: how adversaries can manipulate the learned world models that embodied agents rely on to predict, plan, and act

Outside research, I enjoy jogging, travelling, reading non-fiction and op-eds, and listening to qawwali music.
